The standard

How we grade every unit

Every unit that reaches the shop floor is inspected cosmetically, tested electrically and, where it applies, run mechanically before it's graded. The grade you see on a listing is the same grade defined here — nothing is graded differently for a page that's harder to sell. A "known faults" heading that stays empty is not an oversight; it's the point. If there's nothing to disclose, the section says so.

How we grade — five steps, every time
01
Intake inspection

Serial number logged, unit photographed as received, cosmetic pass begins.

02
Bench test (electrical)

Signal, channel balance and noise floor measured against factory spec.

03
Cosmetic assessment

Every mark measured and mapped — nothing rounded up or hidden by angle.

04
Documentation

Grade, score and known faults written up before a price is even discussed.

05
Publish

Listed with the full report attached — the grade you see is the one we tested to.

The five grades
Mint / near-mint

Indistinguishable from new in normal use, functions exactly to factory spec.

Cosmetic

No visible marks under normal light.

Electrical

Within 0.5dB of factory spec, both channels.

Mechanical

All moving parts smooth, no service needed.

Excellent, light use

Minor cosmetic wear consistent with careful use; performs to spec.

Cosmetic

Light handling marks, not visible at arm's length.

Electrical

Within 1dB of factory spec, both channels.

Mechanical

Serviced within the last 24 months.

Good, honest wear

Visible wear consistent with age; fully functional, faults documented.

Cosmetic

Visible marks or fading, noted with photos.

Electrical

Within 2dB of spec; any channel imbalance disclosed.

Mechanical

May be due for service; timeline noted on listing.

Fair, functional

Noticeable wear or a known limitation; still works, priced accordingly.

Cosmetic

Noticeable wear — dents, fading or missing trim.

Electrical

Functional with a documented quirk (hum, drift, etc.).

Mechanical

Service recommended soon; noted on the listing.

Project unit

Sold as-is for restoration or parts; priced well below working units.

Cosmetic

Significant wear; photographed in full, no angle avoided.

Electrical

May not power on, or a known fault, stated plainly.

Mechanical

Sold as-is; no service performed before listing.

Why we publish a "known faults" heading even when it's empty: a section that only ever appears when there's bad news to share isn't honest — it's a warning light. Ours shows on every listing, faults or none, so its absence means something.
